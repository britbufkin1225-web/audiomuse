## A-Z

- **Apparent Space and Affect** (`apparent-space-and-affect`) — Hold one source constant and vary apparent distance, reverberation, early reflections, and stereo width, collecting ratings for intimacy, size, and immersion separately from any affective report.

- **Chopped Temporal Reconstruction** (`chopped-temporal-reconstruction`) — Compare one source against seven temporal reconstructions of it — regular chopping, irregular chopping, retriggering, stutter, reversed fragments, and inserted silence — so that the Phase 12E temporal-DSP layer is tested as a set of related operations rather than one at a time.

- **Expectation Violation Endings** (`expectation-violation-endings`) — Hold a musical sequence constant and change only its final event — expected, delayed, omitted, harmonically unexpected, or rhythmically displaced — so that five kinds of expectation event can be compared against one baseline.

- **Frequency and Room Position Listening Test** (`frequency-room-position`) — Hear how perceived low-frequency level changes with frequency and listening position in a room.

- **Harmonicity versus Roughness** (`harmonicity-versus-roughness`) — Vary harmonicity and roughness independently in controlled tone complexes and collect separate ratings for fusion, roughness, and pleasantness, so that a perceptual result and an aesthetic one cannot be recorded as the same judgement.

- **Near-Frequency Beating** (`near-frequency-beating`) — Hear and observe the amplitude fluctuation produced when two nearby frequencies are combined.

- **Stutter Rate Continuum** (`stutter-rate-continuum`) — Take one fragment and shorten its repeat period continuously, recording where a listener stops hearing discrete repetition and starts hearing rhythm, flutter, roughness, and finally pitch.

- **Temporal Gating and Expectation** (`temporal-gating-and-expectation`) — Separate gate rate, duty cycle, gate regularity, and dropout placement as four independent manipulations of one sustained source, and collect ratings that keep perceptual and affective reports apart.

- **Timbre and Affect Separation** (`timbre-emotion-separation`) — Hold melody, harmony, and tempo fixed while changing only envelope, brightness, distortion, and spectral noise, to test whether affective ratings shift when nothing about the notes has changed.

- **Waveform and Timbre Comparison** (`waveform-timbre-comparison`) — Compare how sine, triangle, square, and sawtooth signals at one fundamental differ in sound, waveform shape, and spectrum.
