## A-Z

- **ADSR Envelope** (`adsr-envelope`) — A four-stage model of how a sound's amplitude changes over time: attack, decay, sustain, and release.

- **Absorption** (`absorption`) — Conversion of acoustic energy into small amounts of heat within a material.

- **Aesthetic Judgment** (`aesthetic-judgment`) — A subjective evaluation of music as art against a listener's own criteria, proposed as a mechanism distinct from the everyday emotion-induction routes.

- **Affect** (`affect`) — An umbrella term covering evaluative states generally, including emotion, mood, preference, and aesthetic judgement, used when a more precise term is not intended.

- **Affective Timbre** (`affective-timbre`) — The relationship between spectral and envelope properties and the affective qualities listeners attribute to a sound, in which distinct descriptor combinations relate to distinct affective dimensions.

- **Amplitude** (`amplitude`) — The magnitude of a vibration or pressure variation.

- **Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC)** (`analog-to-digital-converter`) — A circuit that converts an analog voltage into numerical samples.

- **Appraisal** (`appraisal`) — Evaluation of an event's significance for the listener, which mediates between a stimulus and an emotional response and explains why the same stimulus affects different people differently.

- **Arousal** (`arousal`) — Physical activation of the autonomic nervous system, one component of an emotional response which can also occur without any emotion at all.

- **Audio Descriptor** (`audio-descriptor`) — A number computed from a signal that describes a property of that signal and nothing about a listener.

- **Audio Signal** (`audio-signal`) — A changing electrical or numerical representation corresponding to sound.

- **Auditory Looming** (`auditory-looming`) — A perceptual asymmetry favouring approaching over receding sound sources, demonstrated with spectral cues while overall intensity is held constant.

- **Auditory Scene Analysis** (`auditory-scene-analysis`) — The process by which a mixture of sound is organized into separate perceived sources, using cues including harmonicity, common onset, spatial position, and continuity.

- **Auditory Transduction** (`auditory-transduction`) — The conversion of airborne pressure variation into neural activity by the ear, in which the cochlea decomposes the signal by frequency along its length before anything is computed.

- **Autobiographical Salience** (`autobiographical-salience`) — How strongly a piece of music is bound to a listener's own past, measured separately from familiarity and shown to have effects beyond what familiarity alone predicts.

- **Autonomic Response** (`autonomic-response`) — Involuntary bodily change such as altered heart rate, skin conductance, respiration, or pupil size, which is evidence of a response without being equivalent to a felt emotion.

- **Beat** (`beat`) — The perceived unit of musical time that listeners count, follow, and move to.

- **Beat Perception** (`beat-perception`) — Inference of a regular underlying pulse from a rhythmic pattern, associated in imaging work with basal ganglia and supplementary motor area activity even without movement.

- **Beatgrid** (`beatgrid`) — A digital coordinate system for musical time that records where a track's beats fall.

- **Beatmatching** (`beatmatching`) — Aligning the tempo and beat positions of two recordings so that they can play together in time.

- **Bit Depth** (`bit-depth`) — The number of bits used to represent each sample's amplitude.

- **Brain Stem Reflex** (`brain-stem-reflex`) — A proposed fast, automatic, unlearned response to acoustic properties signalling an urgent event, described as triggered by sounds that are sudden, loud, or dissonant, or that feature accelerating patterns.

- **Breakbeat** (`breakbeat`) — A rhythmic break isolated from a record and extended or repeated; Session 3 describes doing this by alternating two copies of the same release.

- **Cassette Duplication** (`cassette-duplication`) — The copying of a recorded cassette master onto further cassettes, which made small-scale independent distribution possible without a pressing plant or minimum order.

- **Cassette Mixtape** (`cassette-mixtape`) — A continuous DJ-assembled program recorded to cassette and circulated as a release in its own right rather than as a promotional item.

- **Chopped and Screwed** (`chopped-and-screwed`) — A DJ practice combining sustained reduction of playback rate with manual repetition of beats, words, and phrases, producing a recomposed version of a record rather than a slower copy of it.

- **Chopped and Slowed** (`chopped-and-slowed`) — Later software-based slowing and slicing of existing recordings, related to chopped and screwed practice by technique and terminology but produced by offline editing rather than by live performance.

- **Chopping** (`chopping`) — Manual re-striking of a phrase, word, or beat during playback so that selected material recurs, producing rhythmic interruption the source recording does not contain.

- **Compression (Acoustic)** (`compression-acoustic`) — A region where particles are temporarily crowded and pressure rises above equilibrium.

- **Consonance** (`consonance`) — A perceived quality of stability or compatibility among tones, shaped by acoustics and culture.

- **Critical Band** (`critical-band`) — The frequency region within which components interact rather than being resolved separately, which sets where close-spaced partials produce roughness instead of two distinct pitches.

- **Crossfader** (`crossfader`) — A mixer control that moves audibility between two channels, and in turntablism a gate deciding whether manipulated audio is heard at all.

- **Custom Tape** (`custom-tape`) — A tape recorded to order for a named buyer, often naming that person during the session, so the copy is unique rather than one unit of an edition.

- **DJing** (`djing`) — The live practice of selecting recorded music and controlling how it is cued, aligned, layered, and transitioned for an audience.

- **Detuning** (`detuning`) — Deliberately offsetting one sound source slightly in pitch from another so the two interact instead of matching exactly.

- **Diffraction** (`diffraction`) — Bending and spreading of waves around obstacles and through openings.

- **Digital Signal Processing (DSP)** (`digital-signal-processing`) — Mathematical manipulation of sampled signals.

- **Digital Vinyl System (DVS)** (`digital-vinyl-system`) — A DJ system in which timecode records played on ordinary turntables control the playback of digital audio files.

- **Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC)** (`digital-to-analog-converter`) — A circuit that converts numerical samples into a continuous electrical signal.

- **Direct-to-Reverberant Ratio** (`direct-to-reverberant-ratio`) — The relationship between the level of sound arriving directly from a source and the level of its reflected energy, one of the primary cues from which listeners infer distance.

- **Dissonance** (`dissonance`) — A perceived quality of tension, roughness, or instability among sounds.

- **Dropout** (`dropout`) — Sudden removal of expected material, whose effect depends on the strength of the prediction it breaks rather than on its own duration.

- **Dubbing** (`dubbing`) — Copying recorded material from one tape to another, producing a second physical object whose audio is one generation removed from its source.

- **Duplicate-Record Technique** (`duplicate-record-technique`) — Playing two copies of the same record on separate decks so a passage can be replayed, extended, or interrupted without waiting for the record to return to that point.

- **Duty Cycle** (`duty-cycle`) — The proportion of each gate period during which the signal is open, which determines how much of the source survives and whether the result reads as continuous material with holes or as separate events.

- **Dynamic Range** (`dynamic-range`) — The span between the quietest and loudest parts of a signal, which determines how much room exists for contrast and therefore for dynamic surprise.

- **Early Reflections** (`early-reflections`) — The first discrete reflections arriving after the direct sound, whose pattern and delay contribute to impressions of room size and source distance before the diffuse reverberant field develops.

- **Emotion Measurement** (`emotion-measurement`) — The set of methods used to record affective response — self-report, behavioural, physiological, and neural — none of which is a gold standard and none of which is interchangeable with another.

- **Emotional Contagion** (`emotional-contagion`) — A proposed mechanism in which a listener perceives an emotional expression in music and internally mimics it, drawing on responses to voice-like features.

- **Enculturation** (`enculturation`) — Acquisition of a musical culture's regularities through exposure, which shapes what a listener expects, hears as structured, and finds pleasant.

- **Entrainment** (`entrainment`) — Alignment of an internal periodic process with an external periodicity, used both for bodily and motor alignment and, more contentiously, for neural synchronization.

- **Entropy** (`entropy`) — A measure of the uncertainty of a prediction before the next event is heard, maximal when all continuations are equally likely and minimal when one dominates.

- **Episodic Memory** (`episodic-memory`) — Memory for a specific personal event, which music can cue, bringing the emotion associated with the event along with the recollection.

- **Evaluative Conditioning** (`evaluative-conditioning`) — A proposed mechanism in which a piece acquires affective value through repeated pairing with something positive or negative, described as subconscious, unintentional, and effortless.

- **Expectancy** (`expectancy`) — The anticipation a listener forms about how music will continue, built from exposure to a style, which the music can confirm, delay, omit, or violate.

- **Expressed Emotion** (`expressed-emotion`) — The emotion a performer intends to communicate, distinct from both what a listener perceives and what a listener feels.

- **Familiarity** (`familiarity`) — How well a listener knows a piece of music, which affects neural tracking, physiological response, and rated experience, and which confounds any listening test that does not control it.

- **Felt Emotion** (`felt-emotion`) — The emotion a listener actually experiences in response to music, as distinct from the emotion they hear it as expressing.

- **Filter** (`filter`) — A system that changes selected frequency components of a signal.

- **Fluctuation Strength** (`fluctuation-strength`) — The sensation produced by slow envelope variation, peaking near a 4 Hz modulation rate, measured in vacil and distinct from roughness by rate rather than by kind.

- **Frequency** (`frequency`) — The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz).

- **Frisson** (`frisson`) — A transient peak response reported as chills, shivers, tingling, or piloerection, accompanied by autonomic change and used in research as a marker of intense response.

- **Fundamental Frequency** (`fundamental-frequency`) — The lowest periodic component strongly associated with a sound's perceived pitch.

- **Gate Rate** (`gate-rate`) — How often a gate opens per unit time, which determines whether the result is heard as phrasing, as rhythm, or as timbral modulation.

- **Gating** (`gating`) — Opening and closing a signal path according to a separate control signal, so that material present in the source is heard only during the open intervals.

- **Generational Loss** (`generational-loss`) — Cumulative degradation introduced each time analog material is copied, so a copy of a copy carries more noise and less detail than the master.

- **Grain** (`grain`) — A single short windowed segment of audio used as the unit of granular processing, short enough that its window shape substantially determines its spectrum.

- **Grain Density** (`grain-density`) — The rate at which grains are scheduled, which determines whether the result is heard as a sequence of small events, as texture, or as a pitch at the grain rate.

- **Granular Fragmentation** (`granular-fragmentation`) — Decomposition of a signal into very short windowed segments that can be repositioned, repeated, overlapped, and randomized independently of the source's own structure.

- **Groove** (`groove`) — The perceived rhythmic feel created by small timing, velocity, and accent relationships rather than by exact grid positions.

- **Harmonic** (`harmonic`) — A frequency component at an integer multiple of a fundamental.

- **Harmonicity** (`harmonicity`) — The degree to which a sound's partials approximate integer multiples of a common fundamental, which governs whether components fuse into one perceived source or separate into several.

- **Houston Rap** (`houston-rap`) — The rap economy and set of practices centered on Houston, built on independent labels, local studios, neighborhood networks, and physical distribution rather than on major-label investment.

- **Information Content** (`information-content`) — A measure of how unexpected a particular event is in its context, distinct from how uncertain the prediction was beforehand.

- **Inter-Onset Interval** (`inter-onset-interval`) — The time between the starts of two successive events, which is the quantity a listener uses to infer pulse and the quantity temporal displacement alters.

- **Latency** (`latency`) — Delay between an input event and the corresponding output.

- **MIDI** (`midi`) — A standard protocol that carries musical control instructions such as note, velocity, and channel rather than recorded audio.

- **Masking** (`masking`) — Reduced audibility of one sound because another occupies nearby time or frequency regions.

- **Medium** (`medium`) — Matter through which a mechanical wave travels, such as air, water, wood, or steel.

- **Micro-Looping** (`micro-looping`) — Looping of a segment short enough that the loop period itself becomes a perceptual variable, carrying material from rhythm through flutter and buzz toward perceived pitch as the period shortens.

- **Modulation Rate** (`modulation-rate`) — The frequency at which a signal property varies periodically, which determines the perceptual category of the result: pulsation at low rates, roughness in a middle band, and resolved sidebands above it.

- **Mood** (`mood`) — An affective state lower in intensity than an emotion, without a clear object, and lasting far longer — hours to days rather than minutes.

- **Music Emotion Recognition** (`music-emotion-recognition`) — The machine-learning task of predicting emotion labels for music from audio features and annotated training data, which infers labels rather than detecting feeling.

- **Music and Memory** (`music-and-memory`) — The relationship in which a particular recording carries a personal association strong enough to produce a response that none of its acoustic properties would predict.

- **Music-Evoked Emotion** (`music-evoked-emotion`) — An emotional response arising in a listener during or after listening, produced by several distinguishable mechanisms acting on a combination of music, listener, and situation rather than by a property of the recording.

- **Musical Consonance** (`musical-consonance`) — A judgement that simultaneous tones belong together, arising from spectral fusion, peripheral interaction, and learned exposure to a tonal system rather than from any one of them.

- **Musical Dissonance** (`musical-dissonance`) — A judgement that simultaneous tones do not belong together or require resolution, combining peripheral sensory contributions with learned expectations, and carrying no fixed emotional value.

- **Onset Density** (`onset-density`) — The number of perceptual event starts per unit time, which temporal operations change directly and which determines how busy a passage is independently of its level.

- **Oscillator** (`oscillator`) — A circuit or module that produces a continuous periodic signal used as a sound source at a chosen pitch.

- **Overtone** (`overtone`) — Any frequency component above the fundamental; it may be harmonic or inharmonic.

- **Perceived Emotion** (`perceived-emotion`) — The emotion a listener hears music as expressing, which can be reported without the listener feeling it.

- **Perceived Space** (`perceived-space`) — The impression of location, distance, size, and enclosure that a listener infers from timing, level, spectral, and reverberant cues rather than sensing directly.

- **Perceptual Accent** (`perceptual-accent`) — Prominence arising from temporal context alone, with pitch and loudness held constant, such as an onset not closely followed by another onset being heard as accented.

- **Phase** (`phase`) — A wave's position within its cycle relative to another reference.

- **Phase Locking** (`phase-locking`) — The tendency of auditory nerve fibres to fire preferentially at particular phases of a low-frequency waveform, providing a temporal frequency code that operates up to about 3 kHz in a one-to-one fashion.

- **Phrase Matching** (`phrase-matching`) — Aligning two recordings at the level of musical sections and phrase boundaries rather than only at individual beats.

- **Phrase Repetition** (`phrase-repetition`) — Recurrence of a musical phrase rather than of a single beat, which creates a structural expectation the following phrase can satisfy or refuse.

- **Piloerection** (`piloerection`) — Involuntary raising of body hair, sometimes accompanying frisson, whose status as definitional or merely associated is disputed in the literature.

- **Pitch Shifting** (`pitch-shifting`) — Digital modification of a signal's pitch without a corresponding change in duration, commonly realized as time stretching followed by resampling back to the original length.

- **Prediction Error** (`prediction-error`) — The difference between what was predicted and what arrived, proposed as the quantity that drives learning and reward responses rather than the event itself.

- **Pressure Wave** (`pressure-wave`) — Alternating changes in pressure that propagate through a medium.

- **Probabilistic Prediction** (`probabilistic-prediction`) — Generating expectations about upcoming events as a distribution over possibilities rather than as a single guess, proposed as the second process underlying enculturation.

- **Programme Loudness** (`programme-loudness`) — A standardized algorithmic measure of the loudness of an audio programme, specified by ITU-R Recommendation BS.1770 alongside true-peak level.

- **Psychoacoustics** (`psychoacoustics`) — The study of relationships between physical sound and auditory perception.

- **Pulse** (`pulse`) — The repeating underlying beat a listener perceives as music's steady time reference.

- **Quantization (Timing)** (`quantization-timing`) — Alignment of musical events to exact positions on a timing grid.

- **Rarefaction** (`rarefaction`) — A region where particles spread apart and pressure falls below equilibrium.

- **Reflection** (`reflection`) — Sound energy that returns from a boundary instead of being fully absorbed or transmitted.

- **Regional Distribution** (`regional-distribution`) — The movement of physical recordings from a label to stores within and beyond a region through wholesalers, one-stops, and direct sale, independent of major-label distribution.

- **Repetition** (`repetition`) — The deliberate recurrence of a musical unit, which builds listener expectation that can then be fulfilled, delayed, modified, interrupted, or violated.

- **Resampling** (`resampling`) — Reading stored samples at a different rate than they were recorded at, which scales duration and frequency together and is the digital counterpart of mechanical varispeed.

- **Resonance** (`resonance`) — A strong response when a system is excited near one of its natural frequencies.

- **Retriggering** (`retriggering`) — Restarting a sample or event before it completes, truncating its envelope and replacing it with a new attack, which raises onset density and changes apparent identity without editing the source.

- **Reverberation** (`reverberation`) — The dense persistence of reflected sound after the source stops.

- **Rhythm** (`rhythm`) — The pattern of sonic events and silences through time, including repetition, emphasis, and variation.

- **Room Mode** (`room-mode`) — A standing-wave resonance determined by a room's dimensions and boundaries.

- **Roughness** (`roughness`) — A sensation produced by rapid envelope variation within a critical band, occupying a bounded range of modulation rates and peaking near 70 Hz for a 1 kHz carrier.

- **Salience** (`salience`) — The degree to which an event captures attention, which temporal and dynamic operations change directly and which is separable from whether the event is liked.

- **Sample** (`sample`) — One numerical measurement of signal amplitude at a particular moment.

- **Sample Rate** (`sample-rate`) — The number of samples captured or reproduced per second.

- **Scratching** (`scratching`) — Manual manipulation of a record's playback position, velocity, and direction by hand, often articulated through amplitude gating at the mixer.

- **Screw Music** (`screw-music`) — Community terminology for the slowed and chopped Houston tape tradition, used for the music as a body of work rather than for the technique alone.

- **Screw Tape** (`screw-tape`) — A cassette of slowed, chopped, and often freestyle-carrying material recorded by DJ Screw and sold directly to listeners, functioning at once as recording, performance document, and commodity.

- **Screwed Up Click** (`screwed-up-click`) — The Houston network of rappers who freestyled on DJ Screw's tapes and organized around his sessions, a social network rather than a signed group.

- **Sensorimotor Synchronization** (`sensorimotor-synchronization`) — Coordination of movement with a perceived external rhythm, such as tapping to a beat, which is measurable directly and does not require any claim about neural oscillation.

- **Sensory Dissonance** (`sensory-dissonance`) — The component of perceived dissonance attributable to interaction between spectral components in the auditory periphery, principally roughness, considered apart from learned musical judgements.

- **Sequencer** (`sequencer`) — A device or program that stores and plays back ordered musical events so patterns repeat and stay editable.

- **Silence Insertion** (`silence-insertion`) — Deliberate placement of an interval containing nothing, treated as a positioned element that creates accent, boundary, and contrast rather than as leftover space.

- **Slowed Playback** (`slowed-playback`) — Sustained reduction of the rate at which a recording is played back, which lengthens duration, lowers pitch, and slows tempo together because all three follow from one rate change.

- **Sound** (`sound`) — Mechanical energy traveling through matter as a pressure or structural disturbance.

- **South Park Coalition** (`south-park-coalition`) — A Houston rap collective organized around the South Park neighborhood by K-Rino, functioning as a network of independently releasing artists rather than as a label roster.

- **Spectral Centroid** (`spectral-centroid`) — The amplitude-weighted mean frequency of a spectrum, a descriptor that correlates with perceived brightness without being identical to it.

- **Spectral Flux** (`spectral-flux`) — A measure of how much a spectrum changes between successive analysis frames, which in one EEG study predicted neural synchronization to music better than the amplitude envelope did.

- **Spectral Fusion** (`spectral-fusion`) — The perceptual merging of concurrent components into a single source, strongest for simple integer ratios, demonstrated across listeners from very different musical cultures.

- **Spectrum** (`spectrum`) — The distribution of a sound's energy across frequency.

- **Speech-to-Song Illusion** (`speech-to-song-illusion`) — A perceptual transformation in which an unaltered spoken phrase comes to be heard as sung after exact repetition, and which does not occur if the repetitions are transposed or the syllables jumbled.

- **Standing Wave** (`standing-wave`) — A stable interference pattern created by waves traveling in opposing directions.

- **Statistical Learning** (`statistical-learning`) — Acquisition of a musical system's regularities through ordinary exposure rather than instruction, proposed as one of the two processes underlying enculturation.

- **Stem Separation** (`stem-separation`) — Extraction of components such as vocals, drums, bass, or instrumental parts from an already mixed recording.

- **Stutter** (`stutter`) — Rapid repetition of a short fragment at a period set independently of the source material, so that the repeat rate rather than the fragment content becomes the dominant perceptual variable.

- **Surprise** (`surprise`) — The listener-side experience of an event that the current model rated as unlikely, associated in reviewed work with higher arousal and lower valence.

- **Swing** (`swing`) — A deliberate change of subdivision timing that makes consecutive subdivisions unequal rather than evenly spaced.

- **Syncopation** (`syncopation`) — Systematic placement of accents away from the positions an inferred metre predicts, which contradicts the metre without displacing it.

- **Synthesizer** (`synthesizer`) — An electronic instrument that generates and shapes sound rather than reproducing a recording.

- **Tempo** (`tempo`) — The speed of the beat, describing how quickly musical time passes.

- **Temporal DSP** (`temporal-dsp`) — The family of signal operations that restructure material in time rather than in frequency, including gating, chopping, stuttering, looping, retriggering, dropout, displacement, and granular fragmentation.

- **Temporal Discontinuity** (`temporal-discontinuity`) — An abrupt break in signal continuity that functions at once as a spectral event, a grouping boundary, and a failure of prediction.

- **Temporal Displacement** (`temporal-displacement`) — Placement of an event earlier or later than the position a listener predicts, at scales from microtiming to phrase-level anticipation, which changes felt rhythm without changing which events occur.

- **Third Coast** (`third-coast`) — A contested regional term for Texas, the Gulf Coast, or the wider South as a third center of American popular music, whose boundaries differ by speaker and context.

- **Timbre** (`timbre`) — The qualities that distinguish sounds sharing similar pitch and loudness.

- **Time Stretching** (`time-stretching`) — Digital modification of a signal's duration without a corresponding change in pitch, separating two properties that mechanical rate change moves together.

- **Timecode Vinyl** (`timecode-vinyl`) — A record whose groove carries a control signal instead of music, so that its motion can be read as playback position and speed.

- **Tonotopy** (`tonotopy`) — Organization by frequency along a physical axis, established mechanically in the cochlea and retained at every level of the central auditory system.

- **Transducer** (`transducer`) — A device that converts energy from one form to another.

- **Transmission** (`transmission`) — Sound energy passing through a material or structure into another space.

- **Turntable** (`turntable`) — A mechanical playback system in which a stylus tracks the groove of a rotating record, and in DJ practice a hand-controllable instrument.

- **Turntablism** (`turntablism`) — The practice of performing recorded music by manipulating the turntable and record directly, treating playback hardware as an instrument.

- **Uncertainty** (`uncertainty`) — A listener's lack of confidence about what comes next, distinct from surprise because it describes the state before the event rather than the reaction to it.

- **Valence** (`valence`) — The positive or negative quality of an evaluative state, one of the two dimensions that emotion measures recover most reliably.

- **Varispeed** (`varispeed`) — Continuous adjustment of a playback or record transport's speed, which changes duration and pitch together because both follow from the same transport rate.

- **Vibration** (`vibration`) — Repeated movement around an equilibrium position that can transfer energy into a medium.

- **Vinyl** (`vinyl`) — A record whose spiral groove stores audio as a physical shape that a stylus can trace, and that a performer can move by hand.

- **Waveform** (`waveform`) — A representation of how a signal's amplitude changes over time.

- **Wavelength** (`wavelength`) — The physical length of one cycle; speed of sound divided by frequency.

- **Word Repetition** (`word-repetition`) — Recurrence of a single word or syllable during playback, which holds attention on a semantic unit and delays the line it belongs to while the groove continues.
