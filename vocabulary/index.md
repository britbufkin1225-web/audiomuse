## A-Z

- **ADSR Envelope** (`adsr-envelope`) — A four-stage model of how a sound's amplitude changes over time: attack, decay, sustain, and release.

- **Absorption** (`absorption`) — Conversion of acoustic energy into small amounts of heat within a material.

- **Amplitude** (`amplitude`) — The magnitude of a vibration or pressure variation.

- **Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC)** (`analog-to-digital-converter`) — A circuit that converts an analog voltage into numerical samples.

- **Audio Signal** (`audio-signal`) — A changing electrical or numerical representation corresponding to sound.

- **Beat** (`beat`) — The perceived unit of musical time that listeners count, follow, and move to.

- **Beatgrid** (`beatgrid`) — A digital coordinate system for musical time that records where a track's beats fall.

- **Beatmatching** (`beatmatching`) — Aligning the tempo and beat positions of two recordings so that they can play together in time.

- **Bit Depth** (`bit-depth`) — The number of bits used to represent each sample's amplitude.

- **Breakbeat** (`breakbeat`) — A rhythmic break isolated from a record and extended or repeated; Session 3 describes doing this by alternating two copies of the same release.

- **Cassette Duplication** (`cassette-duplication`) — The copying of a recorded cassette master onto further cassettes, which made small-scale independent distribution possible without a pressing plant or minimum order.

- **Cassette Mixtape** (`cassette-mixtape`) — A continuous DJ-assembled program recorded to cassette and circulated as a release in its own right rather than as a promotional item.

- **Chopped and Screwed** (`chopped-and-screwed`) — A DJ practice combining sustained reduction of playback rate with manual repetition of beats, words, and phrases, producing a recomposed version of a record rather than a slower copy of it.

- **Chopped and Slowed** (`chopped-and-slowed`) — Later software-based slowing and slicing of existing recordings, related to chopped and screwed practice by technique and terminology but produced by offline editing rather than by live performance.

- **Chopping** (`chopping`) — Manual re-striking of a phrase, word, or beat during playback so that selected material recurs, producing rhythmic interruption the source recording does not contain.

- **Compression (Acoustic)** (`compression-acoustic`) — A region where particles are temporarily crowded and pressure rises above equilibrium.

- **Consonance** (`consonance`) — A perceived quality of stability or compatibility among tones, shaped by acoustics and culture.

- **Crossfader** (`crossfader`) — A mixer control that moves audibility between two channels, and in turntablism a gate deciding whether manipulated audio is heard at all.

- **Custom Tape** (`custom-tape`) — A tape recorded to order for a named buyer, often naming that person during the session, so the copy is unique rather than one unit of an edition.

- **DJing** (`djing`) — The live practice of selecting recorded music and controlling how it is cued, aligned, layered, and transitioned for an audience.

- **Detuning** (`detuning`) — Deliberately offsetting one sound source slightly in pitch from another so the two interact instead of matching exactly.

- **Diffraction** (`diffraction`) — Bending and spreading of waves around obstacles and through openings.

- **Digital Signal Processing (DSP)** (`digital-signal-processing`) — Mathematical manipulation of sampled signals.

- **Digital Vinyl System (DVS)** (`digital-vinyl-system`) — A DJ system in which timecode records played on ordinary turntables control the playback of digital audio files.

- **Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC)** (`digital-to-analog-converter`) — A circuit that converts numerical samples into a continuous electrical signal.

- **Dissonance** (`dissonance`) — A perceived quality of tension, roughness, or instability among sounds.

- **Dubbing** (`dubbing`) — Copying recorded material from one tape to another, producing a second physical object whose audio is one generation removed from its source.

- **Duplicate-Record Technique** (`duplicate-record-technique`) — Playing two copies of the same record on separate decks so a passage can be replayed, extended, or interrupted without waiting for the record to return to that point.

- **Filter** (`filter`) — A system that changes selected frequency components of a signal.

- **Frequency** (`frequency`) — The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz).

- **Fundamental Frequency** (`fundamental-frequency`) — The lowest periodic component strongly associated with a sound's perceived pitch.

- **Generational Loss** (`generational-loss`) — Cumulative degradation introduced each time analog material is copied, so a copy of a copy carries more noise and less detail than the master.

- **Groove** (`groove`) — The perceived rhythmic feel created by small timing, velocity, and accent relationships rather than by exact grid positions.

- **Harmonic** (`harmonic`) — A frequency component at an integer multiple of a fundamental.

- **Houston Rap** (`houston-rap`) — The rap economy and set of practices centered on Houston, built on independent labels, local studios, neighborhood networks, and physical distribution rather than on major-label investment.

- **Latency** (`latency`) — Delay between an input event and the corresponding output.

- **MIDI** (`midi`) — A standard protocol that carries musical control instructions such as note, velocity, and channel rather than recorded audio.

- **Masking** (`masking`) — Reduced audibility of one sound because another occupies nearby time or frequency regions.

- **Medium** (`medium`) — Matter through which a mechanical wave travels, such as air, water, wood, or steel.

- **Oscillator** (`oscillator`) — A circuit or module that produces a continuous periodic signal used as a sound source at a chosen pitch.

- **Overtone** (`overtone`) — Any frequency component above the fundamental; it may be harmonic or inharmonic.

- **Phase** (`phase`) — A wave's position within its cycle relative to another reference.

- **Phrase Matching** (`phrase-matching`) — Aligning two recordings at the level of musical sections and phrase boundaries rather than only at individual beats.

- **Phrase Repetition** (`phrase-repetition`) — Recurrence of a musical phrase rather than of a single beat, which creates a structural expectation the following phrase can satisfy or refuse.

- **Pitch Shifting** (`pitch-shifting`) — Digital modification of a signal's pitch without a corresponding change in duration, commonly realized as time stretching followed by resampling back to the original length.

- **Pressure Wave** (`pressure-wave`) — Alternating changes in pressure that propagate through a medium.

- **Psychoacoustics** (`psychoacoustics`) — The study of relationships between physical sound and auditory perception.

- **Pulse** (`pulse`) — The repeating underlying beat a listener perceives as music's steady time reference.

- **Quantization (Timing)** (`quantization-timing`) — Alignment of musical events to exact positions on a timing grid.

- **Rarefaction** (`rarefaction`) — A region where particles spread apart and pressure falls below equilibrium.

- **Reflection** (`reflection`) — Sound energy that returns from a boundary instead of being fully absorbed or transmitted.

- **Regional Distribution** (`regional-distribution`) — The movement of physical recordings from a label to stores within and beyond a region through wholesalers, one-stops, and direct sale, independent of major-label distribution.

- **Repetition** (`repetition`) — The deliberate recurrence of a musical unit, which builds listener expectation that can then be fulfilled, delayed, modified, interrupted, or violated.

- **Resampling** (`resampling`) — Reading stored samples at a different rate than they were recorded at, which scales duration and frequency together and is the digital counterpart of mechanical varispeed.

- **Resonance** (`resonance`) — A strong response when a system is excited near one of its natural frequencies.

- **Reverberation** (`reverberation`) — The dense persistence of reflected sound after the source stops.

- **Rhythm** (`rhythm`) — The pattern of sonic events and silences through time, including repetition, emphasis, and variation.

- **Room Mode** (`room-mode`) — A standing-wave resonance determined by a room's dimensions and boundaries.

- **Sample** (`sample`) — One numerical measurement of signal amplitude at a particular moment.

- **Sample Rate** (`sample-rate`) — The number of samples captured or reproduced per second.

- **Scratching** (`scratching`) — Manual manipulation of a record's playback position, velocity, and direction by hand, often articulated through amplitude gating at the mixer.

- **Screw Music** (`screw-music`) — Community terminology for the slowed and chopped Houston tape tradition, used for the music as a body of work rather than for the technique alone.

- **Screw Tape** (`screw-tape`) — A cassette of slowed, chopped, and often freestyle-carrying material recorded by DJ Screw and sold directly to listeners, functioning at once as recording, performance document, and commodity.

- **Screwed Up Click** (`screwed-up-click`) — The Houston network of rappers who freestyled on DJ Screw's tapes and organized around his sessions, a social network rather than a signed group.

- **Sequencer** (`sequencer`) — A device or program that stores and plays back ordered musical events so patterns repeat and stay editable.

- **Slowed Playback** (`slowed-playback`) — Sustained reduction of the rate at which a recording is played back, which lengthens duration, lowers pitch, and slows tempo together because all three follow from one rate change.

- **Sound** (`sound`) — Mechanical energy traveling through matter as a pressure or structural disturbance.

- **South Park Coalition** (`south-park-coalition`) — A Houston rap collective organized around the South Park neighborhood by K-Rino, functioning as a network of independently releasing artists rather than as a label roster.

- **Spectrum** (`spectrum`) — The distribution of a sound's energy across frequency.

- **Standing Wave** (`standing-wave`) — A stable interference pattern created by waves traveling in opposing directions.

- **Stem Separation** (`stem-separation`) — Extraction of components such as vocals, drums, bass, or instrumental parts from an already mixed recording.

- **Swing** (`swing`) — A deliberate change of subdivision timing that makes consecutive subdivisions unequal rather than evenly spaced.

- **Synthesizer** (`synthesizer`) — An electronic instrument that generates and shapes sound rather than reproducing a recording.

- **Tempo** (`tempo`) — The speed of the beat, describing how quickly musical time passes.

- **Third Coast** (`third-coast`) — A contested regional term for Texas, the Gulf Coast, or the wider South as a third center of American popular music, whose boundaries differ by speaker and context.

- **Timbre** (`timbre`) — The qualities that distinguish sounds sharing similar pitch and loudness.

- **Time Stretching** (`time-stretching`) — Digital modification of a signal's duration without a corresponding change in pitch, separating two properties that mechanical rate change moves together.

- **Timecode Vinyl** (`timecode-vinyl`) — A record whose groove carries a control signal instead of music, so that its motion can be read as playback position and speed.

- **Transducer** (`transducer`) — A device that converts energy from one form to another.

- **Transmission** (`transmission`) — Sound energy passing through a material or structure into another space.

- **Turntable** (`turntable`) — A mechanical playback system in which a stylus tracks the groove of a rotating record, and in DJ practice a hand-controllable instrument.

- **Turntablism** (`turntablism`) — The practice of performing recorded music by manipulating the turntable and record directly, treating playback hardware as an instrument.

- **Varispeed** (`varispeed`) — Continuous adjustment of a playback or record transport's speed, which changes duration and pitch together because both follow from the same transport rate.

- **Vibration** (`vibration`) — Repeated movement around an equilibrium position that can transfer energy into a medium.

- **Vinyl** (`vinyl`) — A record whose spiral groove stores audio as a physical shape that a stylus can trace, and that a performer can move by hand.

- **Waveform** (`waveform`) — A representation of how a signal's amplitude changes over time.

- **Wavelength** (`wavelength`) — The physical length of one cycle; speed of sound divided by frequency.

- **Word Repetition** (`word-repetition`) — Recurrence of a single word or syllable during playback, which holds attention on a semantic unit and delays the line it belongs to while the groove continues.
