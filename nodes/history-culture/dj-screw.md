# DJ Screw

Robert Earl Davis Jr. was born July 20, 1971, in Bastrop, Texas, lived in Smithville and in Los Angeles as a child, and moved to Houston at about the age of ten. The Handbook of Texas records that he began working as a disc jockey in 1989 and often worked on tapes until three or four in the morning; he died on November 16, 2000, of a drug overdose. The University of Houston, which holds his papers, photographs, equipment, and tapes, records that he made and sold tapes from a house on Greenstone Street on the city's south side and produced more than three hundred original mixtapes.

Four qualifications belong in the record rather than in a footnote.

**The nickname is not settled.** The Handbook of Texas derives it from taking two records and screwing them together. The account repeated most often elsewhere has him defacing records he disliked. AudioMuse records both as accounts and neither as established fact; no retrieved source in this phase presents primary documentation for either.

**The invention has no single date.** Sources place his DJ work from 1989 and the mature practice in the early 1990s, and AudioMuse states the emergence as gradual and approximately 1990 to 1992 rather than asserting a first Screw tape. Precision here would be manufactured, not recovered.

**The store claims have different scopes.** The Handbook of Texas places Screwed Up Records & Tapes as a store, label, and business in Houston in 1996. The University of Houston finding aid reports that the store opened in 1998. These may describe business activity and a later storefront rather than incompatible events, so AudioMuse retains both with their attributions. Neither retrieved source establishes February or a Cullen Boulevard address; those details remain unverified.

**June 27 is canonical but reported.** A retrieved Texas Standard history segment dates the freestyle session to June 27, 1996 and identifies DeMo's birthday as the occasion. It remains secondary reporting rather than a primary session record, so AudioMuse records the date and occasion as reported.

The stored edges follow that discipline. `dj-screw --produces--> screw-tape` is direct and uncontested. `dj-screw --enables--> screwed-up-click` reflects that the crew formed around his sessions and tapes. `dj-screw --contributes_to--> chopped-and-screwed` is deliberately not `produces`: institutional sources credit him as the originator of the practice, and AudioMuse says so in prose, while the stored edge leaves room for the Houston DJs and listeners who appear in oral histories of the same period without a verified claim attached. Overstating the edge would convert a strong attribution into a mechanical fact.
