# South Park Coalition

The South Park Coalition matters to AudioMuse for a structural reason rather than a stylistic one. It is the clearest Houston example of a collective that functioned as its own infrastructure: artists organized around a neighborhood, released material themselves, and sustained output for decades without the radio airplay or major-label money that accounts of the group consistently note it did not have. It sits in the gap between the label-driven story of Houston rap and the tape-driven story of DJ Screw, and Phase 12C adds it because a timeline that jumps from Rap-A-Lot to Screw makes the technique look like it emerged from nowhere.

The retrieved chronology supports 1987. A Houston Press retrospective marking twenty-five years in 2012 identifies K-Rino as founder, describes the group as already a few months old at the time of a late-1987 battle, and traces it to crews around Jones and Sterling high schools. This is participant-based journalism rather than an archival founding record, so AudioMuse reports 1987 without promoting it to documentary certainty. The previously mentioned 1986 alternative had no registered support and is not retained as an evidenced position.

Membership is likewise left short. The retrospective names Ganksta N-I-P, Point Blank, Klondike Kat, Street Military, and others, but it describes an informal network whose participation grew through work and loyalty rather than a definitive dated roster. The node therefore names the function of the network and defers exhaustive personnel.
