# South Park Coalition

The South Park Coalition matters to AudioMuse for a structural reason rather than a stylistic one. It is the clearest Houston example of a collective that functioned as its own infrastructure: artists organized around a neighborhood, released material themselves, and sustained output for decades without the radio airplay or major-label money that accounts of the group consistently note it did not have. It sits in the gap between the label-driven story of Houston rap and the tape-driven story of DJ Screw, and Phase 12C adds it because a timeline that jumps from Rap-A-Lot to Screw makes the technique look like it emerged from nowhere.

The founding chronology is not settled here. Accounts give 1986 for K-Rino's founding of the group and 1987 for its formal establishment, and a Houston Press piece marking twenty-five years in 2012 is consistent with the later date. AudioMuse records the range and the disagreement rather than picking one year, and the strongest available anchors are journalistic rather than archival.

Membership is likewise left short. Ganksta N-I-P, Point Blank, Klondike Kat, and Street Military recur across accounts of the collective's early years, but the retrieved sources for this phase do not support a verified roster with dates, so the node names the function of the network and defers the personnel. An exhaustive membership list is explicitly out of scope; what the graph needs is the fact that the network existed and operated independently.
