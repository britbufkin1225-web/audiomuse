# Rap-A-Lot Records

Rap-A-Lot is recorded here as infrastructure. The label is usually described through its artists — the Geto Boys, Scarface, Willie D, Bushwick Bill — but the durable claim for AudioMuse is that a Houston-owned label sustained a working production apparatus: sessions, engineers, arrangers, packaging, and a route to national release that did not require relocating to another city.

The engineering side of that apparatus is visible in participant testimony. Mike Dean describes entering Houston's music scene in the late 1980s and working extensively with the label in the early 1990s alongside N.O. Joe, functioning as both engineer and producer — handling mixing and sonic decisions while playing and arranging musical material, favoring live instrumentation over direct sampling, and later adopting MPC hardware. That is a description of a house style being built by technical staff, which is why `mike-dean` and `houston-studio-infrastructure` sit next to this node rather than under it.

The label also seeded the visual layer of Houston's circulation system. Both Brauch brothers were working for Rap-A-Lot when outside requests for album artwork began arriving, and the design firm they founded went on to shape how Southern rap records looked in retail racks nationally. A label that trains its own engineers and its own designers is producing infrastructure, not only records.

Two claims are deliberately absent. This node states no founding year, because the retrieved sources for this phase do not establish one, and it states nothing about the label's business controversies, which are outside AudioMuse's subject and would require a different evidentiary standard entirely.
