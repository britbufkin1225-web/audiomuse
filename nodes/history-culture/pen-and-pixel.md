# Pen and Pixel

The conceptual claim is that visual packaging is part of how music circulates:

```text
MUSIC -> PHYSICAL MEDIA -> VISUAL PACKAGING -> STORE RECOGNITION -> REGIONAL IDENTITY -> DISTRIBUTION
```

Shawn Brauch as designer and Aaron Brauch as president founded the firm after working for Rap-A-Lot Records, where outside requests for artwork began arriving. The operation grew from an apartment to a five-thousand-square-foot Houston office park facility by 1995, produced roughly twenty thousand covers, and closed in 2003, a closure attributed to file sharing and to post-2001 travel disruption. Its clients extended well beyond Houston to No Limit, Cash Money, and Suave House artists, which is why this node also functions as a cross-regional link: a single Houston shop supplied the visual grammar of independent Southern rap across several cities.

The founding year is disputed. The retrieved interview-based account gives 1993; other accounts give 1992. The cover count is likewise approximate, appearing as roughly twenty thousand in one account and as a precise-looking 19,180 in another. AudioMuse records both disagreements and adopts neither figure as exact.

For an audio atlas the useful abstraction is that a record in a rack has to be found before it can be heard. A distinctive, immediately legible cover performed a discovery function that radio and press were not performing for these labels, and it did so at the exact point in the chain where a buyer was standing in front of the product. That is a circulation mechanism, and it is why the edge runs into `music-distribution`.
