# Southwest Wholesale

A distributor is the part of a music economy that leaves the least trace in the music itself and determines the most about who hears it. Southwest Wholesale operated in Houston as an independent, rap-focused distributor for twenty-seven years and was among the largest in that field. Contemporary trade reporting records that layoffs began on January 30, 2003, that the owner, Robert Guillerman, was quoted trying to keep the business open, and that the collapse was attributed at the time to internet distribution and bootlegging.

That is the extent of what this phase verified. Founding year, founders, address, catalog size, and revenue appear in accounts that could not be retrieved, so they are recorded in the source note as reported and are deliberately absent from this node. The frequently repeated claim that the company opened in 1976 as a one-stop is plausible and consistent with a twenty-seven-year run ending in 2003, and AudioMuse still declines to state it as fact on that basis, because arithmetic consistency is not evidence.

The node earns its place because it names the mechanism. An independent Houston label with no major-label deal still needed a way to get boxes into stores in other cities, and a wholesaler is that way. When it failed, the failure was structural rather than musical: the same records existed, and the route to the shelf did not.
