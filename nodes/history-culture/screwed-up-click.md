# Screwed Up Click

The S.U.C. is recorded as a network because that is what the evidence describes: rappers who came to sessions, freestyled while Screw recorded, and appeared across tapes without a contract defining membership. The Handbook of Texas states that Screw organized the crew; the University of Houston's account of its holdings names participants who freestyled while he recorded, including C-Note, Will-Lean, the Botany Boyz, Lil' Flip, Big Pokey, Big Hawk, Big Moe, Fat Pat, and Lil' Keke. Phase 12C does not attempt a complete membership list, and treats any such list as a claim requiring its own evidence.

One event is recorded here because it is load-bearing for the network's history rather than for its discography. Patrick Lamont Hawkins — Fat Pat — was born December 4, 1970 in Houston, was one of the original members of the Screwed Up Click, and was fatally shot in Houston on February 3, 1998 after visiting a promoter's apartment to collect payment for a performance. His debut album was released two weeks after his death. His older brother, John Edward Hawkins, known as Big Hawk, was shot and killed in 2006. Those are the facts the Handbook of Texas supports, and AudioMuse states them without speculation about circumstances, motive, or unsolved-case detail. What follows from them historically is that a large part of this network's later output and audience relationship is memorial: tapes and freestyles became the surviving record of people who died young, which is one reason the tapes were archived and reissued rather than discarded.

The freestyle practice itself is what connects this node to technique. A Screw tape is not only a DJ mix with rapping over it; the sessions produced material that exists nowhere else, which is why `screwed-up-click --contributes_to--> screw-tape` runs in that direction. The tape is the artifact; the network supplied part of what the artifact contains.
