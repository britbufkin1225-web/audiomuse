# Musical Expectation

Session 2 already gave AudioMuse the mechanism in plain terms: the auditory system recognizes repetition, and once a pattern repeats the listener forms an expectation, which can then be fulfilled, delayed, modified, interrupted, or completely violated. That statement is the seam where the existing music foundation joins the Phase 12E affective material, and `repetition` holds the compositional half of it.

Juslin names musical expectancy as one of the eight BRECVEMA mechanisms, describing it as an emotion induced when a feature of the music violates, delays, or confirms expectations built from the listener's previous experience of the same style, and credits Meyer with the argument. Note what that definition contains: the expectation is style-specific and learned, so the same event is a violation only relative to a particular listening history. `musical-enculturation` holds where the history comes from.

Pearce's review supplies the quantities. Information content measures how surprising a particular event was; entropy measures how uncertain the prediction was beforehand. Keeping them apart matters musically, because a passage that keeps the listener unsure what is coming and a passage that delivers something unforeseen are different experiences, and a single word like tension covers both.

The affective consequence is not a simple ranking. Pearce reports high-information-content passages associated with higher arousal and lower valence, and states that the proposed inverted U between predictability and pleasure has support in some studies and not others. Witek and colleagues found the inverted U clearly for syncopation, movement, and pleasure, with medium syncopation rated highest and both extremes lower, and proposed that medium syncopation balances enough predictability for metre to be inferred against enough complexity for expectation to be violated. That is a specific result in a specific domain rather than a general law about surprise.

What Phase 12E adds to this node is the temporal-DSP layer beneath it. A delay, an omission, and a displacement are all expectation events, and they can be produced by a crossfader, a gate, or a sampler rather than by a chord. `temporal-discontinuity` is the mechanism; this node is what it acts on.
