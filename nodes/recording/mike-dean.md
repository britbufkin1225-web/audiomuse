# Mike Dean

AudioMuse records Mike Dean as a technical node, not as a biography. The lineage this phase establishes is:

```text
Rap-A-Lot -> Houston studio engineering -> Mike Dean -> Geto Boys and Scarface work -> later production systems
```

In his own account, he entered Houston's music scene in the late 1980s and worked extensively with Rap-A-Lot Records in the early 1990s alongside N.O. Joe. He describes functioning as engineer and producer at once — making mixing and sonic decisions while playing and arranging musical material — preferring live instrumentation and elaboration over direct sampling, and later adopting MPC hardware. He names work with the Geto Boys, Scarface, UGK, and Z-Ro.

Two things make that useful to an audio atlas rather than to a credits database. The first is the combined role: when the person mixing is also the person arranging, mix decisions become compositional decisions, and the resulting record cannot be cleanly separated into "the music" and "the engineering." The second is the instrumentation preference, which is a technical choice with audible consequences — played parts rather than lifted loops change what the low end, the phrasing, and the harmonic movement can do, and it is the choice most often cited to distinguish this material from its contemporaries.

Later work associated with him extends to nationally released production far beyond Houston, including the Travis Scott era of Houston-connected artists. This phase names that continuation without documenting it: the single retrieved interview dates from 2013 and cannot support later claims, and a full production discography is out of scope. What the node holds is the Houston segment of the lineage, with an explicit gap where the later segment would need its own sources.
