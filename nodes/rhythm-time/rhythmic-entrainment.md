# Rhythmic Entrainment

The word carries two claims of very different strength, and AudioMuse separates them deliberately.

The weaker and better-supported claim is about synchronization. Weineck, Wen, and Henry measured EEG responses to tempo-modulated music and found neural activity tracking the stimulus, strongest to the spectral flux of the music rather than to its amplitude envelope, and strongest at stimulation tempi between 1 and 2 Hz — a range they relate to the modal tempo of Western pop music and to natural adult body movement rates. Familiar songs and songs with easier-to-perceive beats produced stronger tracking; enjoyment did not predict it.

The stronger claim is that an endogenous neural oscillator is being entrained. Those same authors decline to make it. They state that they use entrainment in the broad sense without assuming how neural synchronization arises, and they explicitly acknowledge an unresolved debate between phase-locked coupling to a neural oscillator and convolution of the stimulus with the responses it evokes. AudioMuse adopts their position rather than resolving a live methodological dispute in a knowledge atlas.

Motor involvement is a separate and firmer line of evidence. Grahn and Brett found that rhythms with integer-ratio interval relationships and regular perceptual accents were reproduced more accurately and elicited higher activity in the basal ganglia and supplementary motor area, and concluded that these regions may mediate beat perception in addition to producing movement. Their finding that perceptual accent can arise from temporal structure alone — an onset not closely followed by another is heard as accented — is the one Phase 12E leans on most, because it means silence and spacing create accents. `silence-as-musical-material` and `gating` both exploit that.

Juslin lists rhythmic entrainment among the BRECVEMA mechanisms, describing an external rhythm drawing an internal bodily rhythm toward a common periodicity, with the adjusted rhythm then spreading to feeling through proprioceptive feedback. That is the affective route, offered as theory. `groove` holds where entrainment and pleasure have actually been measured together.
