# DJing

A DJ works with recordings the way a performer works with an instrument's material: choosing what plays, deciding where in a track playback begins, aligning one recording's musical time with another's, and controlling what the audience hears during the change between them. AudioMuse therefore treats DJing as a practice applied to recorded sound and musical time, not simply as playback.

Three repository threads meet here. Session 1 places DJ work inside acoustics: phase relationships affect DJ transitions, and cue monitoring, speaker placement, subwoofer alignment, and room modes decide what the DJ actually hears while working. Session 2 supplies musical time, describing a DJ beatgrid as a digital coordinate system for musical time and noting that a DJ reads a track's sections as navigational landmarks, so mixing is often mixing structures rather than mixing tracks. Session 3 supplies the repository's conceptual example of breakbeat DJing: alternating two copies of one record to isolate and extend a rhythmic break. Specific people, places, dates, and origin claims remain session chronology rather than durable claims in this node.

From that practice the turntable stopped being only a playback device, which is where `turntablism` begins. Digital tools later joined the same practice without replacing it: `digital-vinyl-system` keeps the turntable as the control surface, and Session 3 records real-time stem separation as a further extension.

The registered sessions document DJ practice through New York breakbeat DJing, house, techno, and later digital systems, and contain no material on regional Southern United States DJ traditions. Phase 12C filled that gap from registered external sources rather than from the sessions: Houston and Third Coast practice now lives in `chopped-and-screwed`, `slowed-playback`, `chopping`, `screw-tape`, and the `history-culture` domain, and `djing --contributes_to--> screw-tape` records that a Screw tape is a DJ performance before it is anything else. The session layer still says nothing about that material, which is why those nodes cite external provenance instead of session origin.
