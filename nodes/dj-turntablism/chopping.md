# Chopping

Chopping is the discrete half of chopped and screwed. Where slowed playback changes a continuous parameter, chopping intervenes at points: a phrase already heard is struck again, a word is repeated before the line finishes, a beat is doubled. The mechanism is the one the existing `turntablism` node already describes for breakbeat DJing — two copies of the same record, one cued to the point that will be repeated while the other plays — applied to a different musical purpose. Session 3 records that two-copy technique as the means of extending a break indefinitely; chopping uses the same means to interrupt rather than to extend.

The operations involved are familiar from turntablism and are not redefined here: cueing, repositioning the record by hand, backspinning to a marked point, switching between decks, and gating the result with the crossfader so only the intended fragment is heard. What is specific to this practice is the target. A break-extension chop selects a rhythmic bar; a Screw-style chop frequently selects a word or a short vocal phrase, which changes what repetition does to a listener. Repeating a bar sustains a groove; repeating a word insists on it.

The University of Houston's curator names exactly this pairing when describing the practice as slowing the music down and repeating certain beats, words, and phrases. The `repetition` node holds the compositional consequences; this node holds the physical act that produces them, and `chopping --contributes_to--> rhythm` records that the rhythmic result belongs to neither source copy alone.

Because the chop is performed against a moving record, its placement carries the performer's timing. That is a meaningful difference from a software slice, which lands where it is placed and stays there. The comparison is developed on `chopped-and-slowed`.
