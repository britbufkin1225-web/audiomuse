# Auditory Reward

Zatorre and Salimpoor's review is the source AudioMuse uses for this layer, and the finding it reports is a connection rather than a location. Auditory cortical regions, particularly superior temporal gyrus, showed increased functional interaction with the nucleus accumbens when musical sequences carried high rather than low reward value. Their reading is that the systems responsible for pattern analysis and prediction are in greater informational cross-talk with the systems that assign reward value. That is a claim about a relationship between two levels of the stack, which is exactly what a layered model needs and what a brain-region-equals-emotion story cannot supply.

The second reported finding is a dissociation in time. Activity in the dorsal striatum, the caudate, is described during the anticipation phase immediately preceding chills, while nucleus accumbens activity increased with pleasure and was maximal during the chills themselves. Anticipating and having are not the same event, and they are not in the same place. For a producer this is the formal counterpart of something already obvious in practice: the bar before the drop is doing work, and it is not the same work as the drop.

The mechanism the review invokes is prediction. Listeners form expectancies from implicit knowledge acquired through previous exposure, and striatal responses are framed in terms of reward prediction and the difference between what was expected and what arrived. `predictive-processing-music` holds that machinery; `musical-expectation` holds its musical form.

Three cautions belong on the node rather than in a footnote. The evidence is correlational neuroimaging and the authors say so. The authors state that the rewarding nature of aesthetic stimuli is not universal, differing across cultures and between individuals within a culture. And they state that the functional roles of these structures cannot be attributed to any single dimension and are altered by factors not yet all identified. AudioMuse records the finding and keeps all three cautions attached to it.
