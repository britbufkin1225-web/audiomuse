# Musical Enculturation

AudioMuse needs this node to hold a position between two bad options. The claim that musical meaning is universal cannot survive contact with the cross-cultural evidence. The claim that it is entirely cultural cannot survive contact with the perceptual evidence. Both are available in the popular literature and neither is what the research says.

The evidence for the learned side is Pearce's account: exposure builds internal models of statistical regularities without instruction, and prediction from those models organizes how subsequent music is heard. This is why a listener steeped in one style can hear structure in it that another listener, with equal hearing, cannot. It is also why familiarity effects are so persistent across the literature — Weineck and colleagues found stronger neural synchronization to familiar songs, Janata found stronger medial prefrontal response with greater familiarity and autobiographical salience, and Harrison and Loui report that people are more likely to react physically to familiar music.

The evidence for the shared side is McPherson and colleagues. Native Amazonian listeners with limited exposure to Western music fused simple-integer-ratio intervals in the same pattern as Boston listeners, octave strongest in both. That is a perceptual regularity that does not require Western enculturation.

The two findings meet in the same paper, and their meeting is what this node exists to record. Those same Amazonian listeners showed no preference for consonant over dissonant intervals, and in the Western listeners fusion did not predict pleasantness either. The authors conclude that universal perceptual biases exist and could partially constrain musical systems, but that they shape aesthetic responses only indirectly, and aesthetic response is instead determined by what is prevalent in the system an individual experiences.

So the layers coexist. Biology supplies constraints and tendencies; exposure supplies the statistics; the aesthetic response sits with the statistics. Pearce names as an open question what happens to a listener exposed to more than one style during enculturation, which is the ordinary condition of most listeners alive now and a gap AudioMuse should not paper over.
