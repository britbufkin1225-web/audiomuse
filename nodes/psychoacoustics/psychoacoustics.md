# Psychoacoustics

Psychoacoustics studies the relationship between measurable sound and what listeners hear. The auditory system organizes pressure changes into qualities such as pitch, timbre, rhythm, loudness, and location, influenced by context and expectation.

This distinction prevents AudioMuse from treating measurements as complete descriptions of experience. It also connects acoustics to music, recording decisions, DSP, and listening experiments.

Phase 12E answered the standing question about which perceptual effects deserve focused child nodes. `auditory-transduction` and `auditory-pathway` hold the encoding stage; `roughness`, `harmonicity`, and `sensory-dissonance` hold specific perceptual properties; and `affective-psychoacoustics` holds the discipline of relating those properties to affective response without collapsing the steps between them. The relationship this node has always asserted — that measurement does not describe experience — becomes load-bearing there, because the temptation to treat a measurable property as an emotion is strongest exactly where the property has a number attached to it.
