# Autonomic Response to Music

The purpose of this node is one sentence that Phase 12E is not allowed to blur: a physiological change is not an emotion.

Mauss and Robinson's review is the source. Across response systems the evidence favours the view that measures of emotional responding recover dimensions such as valence and arousal rather than discrete emotional states. Experiential, physiological, and behavioural response systems carry unique sources of variance, which limits how strongly they converge with one another, and the authors conclude that there is no gold-standard measure of emotional responding and that the three kinds cannot be assumed interchangeable. They give facial EMG as sensitive to valence and skin conductance as sensitive to arousal — different instruments reading different axes, not three views of one number.

For music this has direct consequences. A rise in skin conductance during a passage is evidence about arousal. It is not evidence that the listener felt tension, or fear, or excitement, and it does not become so by adding an adjective. Equally, a listener reporting a powerful response with no measurable autonomic change has not been contradicted; the measures do not converge that tightly.

Juslin's framework contains the same distinction structurally. Arousal appears in his working definitions as physical activation of the autonomic nervous system, and he notes it can occur in the absence of emotion — exercise being the obvious case. Rhythmic entrainment, in his account, works by an external rhythm drawing an internal bodily rhythm toward a common periodicity and only then spreading to felt emotion through proprioceptive feedback. Even in a framework built to explain feeling, the bodily change is a step on the path and not the destination.

`frisson` is the case where the two come closest, because piloerection and chills are simultaneously physiological and reportable. Even there, Harrison and Loui record disagreement over whether the physiological sign is definitional. `emotion-measurement` holds the resulting rules for AudioMuse listening tests.
