## Supports

- Bill Holford established Audio Company of America in 1948 and opened a dedicated Houston studio in
  1950, moving through a documented sequence of addresses into the 1990s.
- The studio recorded R&B, rock and roll, blues, country, polka, Cajun, zydeco, and classical material,
  including Lightnin' Hopkins and T-Bone Walker.
- Don Robey recorded extensively there for Duke, Peacock, and Songbird.
- Later rooms held tube and ribbon microphones and a large tracking space.

## Why Phase 12C uses it

- It supplies the engineering and room layer of the cartography: a named studio, its operator, its
  clients, and its equipment, rather than an unattributed claim that Houston "had studios."
