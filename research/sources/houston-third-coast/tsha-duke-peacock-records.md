## Supports

- Don Robey established Peacock Records in Houston in 1949, naming it after his Fifth Ward nightclub,
  the Bronze Peacock.
- In April 1953 Robey acquired full control of Duke, founded in Memphis in 1952, with the contracts of
  Bobby "Blue" Bland, Junior Parker, and Johnny Ace.
- In 1953 operations moved from Lyons Avenue to 2809 Erastus Street, the site of the closed club.
- Blues, R&B, and gospel rosters included Big Mama Thornton, Marie Adams, the Dixie Hummingbirds, the
  Sensational Nightingales, and the Mighty Clouds of Joy.
- Robey used Houston's ACA Studios for recording and Gold Star for mastering.
- On May 23, 1973, Robey sold the Duke-Peacock holdings to ABC-Dunhill.

## Why Phase 12C uses it

- It establishes Black-owned label, studio, and catalog infrastructure in Houston decades before the
  rap economy, which is the historical spine the later chronology depends on.
