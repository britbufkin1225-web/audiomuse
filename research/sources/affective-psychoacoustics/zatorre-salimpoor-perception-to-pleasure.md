# From Perception to Pleasure: Music and Its Neural Substrates

- Source ID: `zatorre-salimpoor-perception-to-pleasure`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed review)
- Authors: Robert J. Zatorre, Valorie N. Salimpoor
- Publication: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 110 (Suppl. 2), 10430–10437, 2013
- Identifier: DOI 10.1073/pnas.1301228110; PMCID PMC3690607
- Stable locator: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3690607/
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Auditory cortical regions show increased functional interaction with the nucleus accumbens when
  musical sequences carry high rather than low reward value, linking cortical pattern analysis to
  subcortical reward valuation.
- Listeners form expectancies from implicit knowledge acquired through previous exposure, and
  striatal activity is described in terms of reward prediction and prediction error.
- A dissociation is reported between dorsal striatal (caudate) activity during the anticipation phase
  preceding chills and nucleus accumbens activity maximal during the chills themselves.

## Does not settle

- Causation. The review reports correlational neuroimaging and says so; it does not establish that
  any structure causes a felt emotion.
- Universality. The authors state that the rewarding nature of aesthetic stimuli differs across
  cultures and between individuals within a culture.
- Any claim that a specific chord, key, interval, or tempo produces a specific emotion. The paper
  makes no such claim and must not be cited for one.
- The authors state that the functional roles of these structures are not attributable to any one
  dimension and are altered by factors not all of which have been identified.
