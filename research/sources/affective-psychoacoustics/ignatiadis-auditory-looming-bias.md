# Neural Mechanisms Underlying the Auditory Looming Bias

- Source ID: `ignatiadis-auditory-looming-bias`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Kosmas Ignatiadis, Diana Baier, Brigitta Toth, Robert Baumgartner
- Publication: Auditory Perception and Cognition 4 (1–2), 60–73, 2021
- Identifier: DOI 10.1080/25742442.2021.1977582; PMCID PMC7612677
- Stable locator: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612677/
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- The auditory looming bias is an early perceptual phenomenon in which listeners are more alert to
  approaching auditory objects than to receding ones.
- In this study the bias appeared behaviourally as higher accuracy and faster reaction times for
  looming than receding trials.
- The bias was produced here by manipulating spatial spectral cues while holding overall intensity
  constant, which shows the effect is not solely an artefact of rising loudness.
- The authors describe the evaluation as fast and probably automatic, and report directional
  connectivity differences beginning shortly after stimulus onset.

## Does not settle

- Emotion. Alertness and reaction time are not affective measures, and no claim about threat, fear,
  or tension may rest on this source.
- Causal neural mechanism. The authors state reservations about their connectivity method, note that
  source localization used a template brain, and describe an unresolved gap between single-trial and
  averaged analyses.
- Anything about reverberation, room acoustics, or musical spatialization. Those remain unsourced in
  AudioMuse and are treated as open.
