## Supports

The modulation-rate continuum, which is the psychoacoustic backbone of the Phase 12E stutter and
micro-looping material. Taking a 1 kHz tone amplitude-modulated at 100% depth and raising the
modulation frequency:

- below about 10 Hz the ear tracks the change and hears pulsation or beating;
- fluctuation strength peaks near 4 Hz, and its unit, the vacil, is defined by a 1 kHz tone at 60 dB
  modulated at 4 Hz with modulation depth 1;
- around 20 Hz a distinct slow or R-roughness is described, occurring between roughly 15 and 45 Hz;
- envelope variations between roughly 20 and 300 Hz are perceived as rough;
- roughness peaks near 70 Hz for a 1 kHz carrier and shifts down for lower carrier frequencies;
- the unit of roughness, the asper, is defined by a 1 kHz tone at 60 dB modulated at 70 Hz with
  modulation depth 1;
- above that band the main spectral line and sidebands of a modulated tone become audible as
  individual tones.

It also supports two secondary points AudioMuse needs. Roughness depends on centre frequency,
modulation frequency, and modulation depth, and only weakly on signal level. And the note states
directly that modulated sounds command more attention than unmodulated ones, but that if the listener
is interested in the information the sound conveys, modulation is not perceived as annoying — the
same modulation is judged annoying only when the sound is unwanted.

## Does not settle

- Musical meaning. The note is written for product sound design and vehicle noise, and its
  annoyance judgements are engineering judgements about unwanted noise.
- Any claim that roughness is unpleasant. Its own framing contradicts that reading, which is why it
  is registered here.
- Emotion of any kind. No affective measure appears in it.
- The underlying perceptual research. The values are attributed to the psychoacoustics literature and
  to Sottek's hearing model; AudioMuse has not retrieved those primary works.
