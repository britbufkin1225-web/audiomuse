# Illusory Transformation from Speech to Song

- Source ID: `deutsch-speech-to-song-illusion`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Diana Deutsch, Trevor Henthorn, Rachael Lapidis
- Publication: Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 129 (4), 2245–2252, 2011
- Identifier: DOI 10.1121/1.3562174
- Stable locator: https://deutsch.ucsd.edu/pdf/JASA-2011_129_2245-2252.pdf
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Repetition alone, without altering the signal, can move a spoken phrase from being heard as speech
  to being heard as song. The paper's own framing is that the illusion occurs without training and
  without context from other sounds.
- The effect depends on exact repetition. When intervening presentations were transposed slightly, or
  when the syllables were presented in jumbled orderings, judgments did not move from speech to song.
- In the second experiment, subjects who heard ten presentations repeated the phrase back as song,
  while subjects who heard one presentation repeated it back as speech.
- Repeated renditions were closer to a hypothesized simple tonal melody than to the original spoken
  pitch values, which the authors read as perceptual distortion toward a tonal representation rather
  than as improved accuracy.

## Does not settle

- Anything about emotion. The measure is a speech-to-song rating, not an affective one.
- The number of repetitions required in general. The experiments used one versus ten presentations
  and a ten-presentation rating task; they do not establish a threshold.
- Whether the effect generalizes to musical material rather than speech, or to the very short
  repetition periods used in stutter and micro-looping. AudioMuse treats that extension as a
  hypothesis, not as a finding of this paper.
- The subjects had at least five years of musical training, which bounds generalization.
