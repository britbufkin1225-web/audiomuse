# Measures of Emotion: A Review

- Source ID: `mauss-robinson-measures-of-emotion`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed review)
- Authors: Iris B. Mauss, Michael D. Robinson
- Publication: Cognition and Emotion 23 (2), 209–237, 2009
- Identifier: DOI 10.1080/02699930802204677
- Stable locator: https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930802204677
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Across response systems, the evidence favours the view that measures of emotional responding
  reflect dimensions such as valence and arousal rather than discrete emotional states.
- Experiential, physiological, and behavioural response systems carry unique sources of variance,
  which limits how strongly they converge with one another.
- The authors state there is no gold-standard measure of emotional responding, and that experiential,
  physiological, and behavioural measures cannot be assumed to be interchangeable.
- Different measures are sensitive to different dimensions: the review gives facial EMG as sensitive
  to valence and skin conductance as sensitive to arousal.

## Does not settle

- Anything specific to music. This is a general affective-science review, and AudioMuse uses it for
  the measurement contract rather than for any musical finding.
- Whether discrete emotion categories are wrong as a theory of emotion. The claim concerns what
  measures recover, not what emotions are.
- Which measure to prefer for a given question. The review's point is that the choice is a design
  decision with consequences, not that one instrument wins.
