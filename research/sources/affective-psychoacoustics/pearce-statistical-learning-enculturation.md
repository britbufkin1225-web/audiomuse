# Statistical Learning and Probabilistic Prediction in Music Cognition

- Source ID: `pearce-statistical-learning-enculturation`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed review)
- Author: Marcus T. Pearce
- Publication: Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences 1423 (1), 378–395, 2018
- Identifier: DOI 10.1111/nyas.13654; PMCID PMC6849749
- Stable locator: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6849749/
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Musical enculturation is proposed to rest on two processes: statistical learning of regularities in
  the music a listener is exposed to, and probabilistic prediction from the learned model.
- Information content measures how unexpected an event is in a particular context; entropy measures
  the uncertainty of the prediction before the next event is heard. They are distinct quantities, and
  a passage can be uncertain without being surprising.
- Modelled information content accounted for up to 83% of the variance in listeners' pitch
  expectations across the studies reviewed.
- High-information-content passages correlated with higher subjective and physiological arousal and
  lower valence than low-information-content passages.
- On the proposed inverted-U relationship between predictability and pleasure, the author states that
  it has received empirical support in some but not all studies, and that the reviewed result may
  reflect only one side of such a curve.
- Expectation, emotion, memory, similarity, segmentation, and metre are all framed as consequences of
  probabilistic prediction from learned models.

## Does not settle

- Causation. The author states the reviewed results are correlational rather than causal, with one
  artificial-system exception.
- Timbre, dynamics, and texture. The IDyOM model the review is built on cannot process them, which is
  a stated limitation and is directly relevant to any AudioMuse use in a production context.
- Generality across metres, and the effect of exposure to more than one musical style during
  enculturation; both are named as open questions.
