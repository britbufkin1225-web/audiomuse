# Perception and Modeling of Affective Qualities of Musical Instrument Sounds across Pitch Registers

- Source ID: `mcadams-affective-qualities-instrument-sounds`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Stephen McAdams, Chelsea Douglas, Naresh N. Vempala
- Publication: Frontiers in Psychology 8, 153, 2017
- Identifier: DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00153; PMCID PMC5296353
- Stable locator: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00153
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Distinct combinations of audio descriptors relate to distinct affective dimensions, rather than one
  descriptor carrying affect generally:
  - valence rated more positive with lower spectral slopes, greater emergence of strong partials, and
    an amplitude envelope with sharper attack and earlier decay;
  - tension arousal higher with brighter sounds, more spectral variation, and more gentle attacks;
  - energy arousal higher with brighter sounds, higher spectral centroids, and slower decrease of the
    spectral slope.
- Register interacted with instrument identity: valence ratings had a nonlinear concave relation to
  register, more positive in middle registers, apart from percussion.
- Twenty-three audio descriptors were computed and modelled; the conclusion is that the three emotion
  dimensions are carried by distinct acoustic properties.

## Does not settle

- That timbre determines emotion. The authors make no such claim and the design cannot support one.
- Musical context. The stimuli were isolated tones, and the authors state that future research should
  validate the relationships in a musical context.
- Felt emotion. The task collected affective ratings of sounds, which sits closer to perceived than
  to induced emotion.
- Anything about distortion, saturation, or processed electronic timbres; the corpus was orchestral
  instrument sounds.
