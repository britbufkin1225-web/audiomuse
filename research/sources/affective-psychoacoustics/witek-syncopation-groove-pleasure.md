# Syncopation, Body-Movement and Pleasure in Groove Music

- Source ID: `witek-syncopation-groove-pleasure`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Maria A. G. Witek, Eric F. Clarke, Mikkel Wallentin, Morten L. Kringelbach, Peter Vuust
- Publication: PLOS ONE 9 (4), e94446, 2014
- Identifier: DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0094446
- Stable locator: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094446
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- The relationship between degree of syncopation and both wanting-to-move and pleasure ratings is an
  inverted U rather than a monotonic increase: medium degrees of syncopation elicited the most desire
  to move and the most pleasure.
- Low-syncopation patterns rated higher for movement than high-syncopation patterns, with both below
  the medium condition.
- The authors' proposed explanation: medium syncopation balances enough rhythmic predictability for
  metre to be perceived against enough complexity for expectations to be violated. High syncopation
  may prevent successful entrainment and so inhibit the desire to move.
- Rhythmic complexity in the form of syncopation is identified as characteristic of funk, hip-hop, and
  electronic dance music.

## Does not settle

- Whether the ratings correspond to actual body movement. The authors state that the relationship
  between the ratings recorded and overt movement remains unknown.
- Where the peak of the inverted U sits for any particular genre, listener population, or tempo. The
  study establishes the shape, not a transferable optimum.
- Anything about temporal manipulations other than syncopation. Gating, chopping, stutter, dropout,
  and retriggering were not tested and this source must not be cited for them.
