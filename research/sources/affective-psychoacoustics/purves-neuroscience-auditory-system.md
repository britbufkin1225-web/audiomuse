## Supports

- Mechanoelectrical transduction: deflection of the hair bundle toward the tallest stereocilia opens
  cation-selective transduction channels gated by tip links; potassium entry depolarizes the cell,
  opening voltage-gated calcium channels and triggering transmitter release onto auditory nerve
  endings. Deflection in the opposite direction hyperpolarizes; deflection perpendicular to the plane
  of symmetry produces no response.
- Speed and sensitivity: the text states that hair cells can convert bundle displacement into an
  electrical potential in as little as 10 microseconds, and that hair bundle movements at the
  threshold of hearing are approximately 0.3 nm.
- Frequency coding uses two mechanisms. At low frequencies nerve fibres track the stimulus waveform in
  time; at higher frequencies that breaks down and place coding along the tonotopically organized
  cochlea carries the information.
- Tuning curves and characteristic frequency: the lowest threshold of a fibre's tuning curve is its
  characteristic frequency. Apical fibres respond to low frequencies, basal fibres to high.
- Phase locking: hair cells release transmitter only during the depolarizing phase, so fibres fire
  preferentially at particular phases of low-frequency waveforms. The text states that hair cells and
  auditory nerve fibres can follow stimuli up to about 3 kHz in a one-to-one fashion.
- Tonotopic organization is retained at all levels of the central auditory system.

## Does not settle

- Anything about emotion, reward, or affective response. This is an anatomy and physiology reference.
- Current research positions. The edition retrieved is from 2001, and later work has refined several
  of these accounts; the statements used here are the stable structural ones.
- Any claim about how a perceptual feature becomes an affective judgement.
