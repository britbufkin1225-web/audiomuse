# Neural Synchronization Is Strongest to the Spectral Flux of Slow Music

- Source ID: `weineck-neural-synchronization-spectral-flux`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Kristin Weineck, Olivia Xin Wen, Molly J. Henry
- Publication: eLife 11, e75515, 2022
- Identifier: DOI 10.7554/eLife.75515
- Stable locator: https://elifesciences.org/articles/75515
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Neural synchronization measured with EEG was strongest to the spectral flux of music rather than to
  the amplitude envelope, under both analysis approaches the authors used.
- Strongest synchronization occurred for stimulation tempi between 1 and 2 Hz, which the authors
  relate to the modal tempo of Western pop music and to natural adult body movement rates.
- Familiar songs and songs with an easier-to-perceive beat produced higher response correlations;
  enjoyment did not significantly predict synchronization.
- The terminology caveat AudioMuse needs: the authors state they use entrainment in the broad sense,
  without assuming how neural synchronization arises, and explicitly acknowledge the unresolved debate
  between phase-locked coupling to a neural oscillator and convolution of the stimulus with evoked
  responses.

## Does not settle

- Whether neural entrainment in the strict oscillatory sense occurs. The authors deliberately decline
  to take a position, and AudioMuse must not resolve it on their behalf.
- Any link between neural synchronization and felt emotion. The study measures synchronization,
  familiarity, and beat salience, not affect.
- Whether spectral flux is the best predictor for music outside the tested corpus and tempo range.
