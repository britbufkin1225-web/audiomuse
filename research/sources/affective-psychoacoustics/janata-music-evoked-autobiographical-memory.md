# The Neural Architecture of Music-Evoked Autobiographical Memories

- Source ID: `janata-music-evoked-autobiographical-memory`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Author: Petr Janata
- Publication: Cerebral Cortex 19 (11), 2579–2594, 2009
- Identifier: DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhp008; PMCID PMC2758676
- Stable locator: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2758676/
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Dorsal medial prefrontal cortex showed the strongest evidence of integrating music with memory,
  responding parametrically as the degree of autobiographical association and familiarity increased.
- Familiarity and autobiographical salience were separable: salience showed effects in medial
  prefrontal regions beyond what familiarity alone predicted.
- The study separated musical structure from personal meaning analytically, modelling tonality
  tracking independently of subjective salience, and reports that the two operate on different
  timescales.

## Does not settle

- That the memory rather than the music produces the emotion. The study measures neural response and
  ratings, not emotion causation.
- Anything about nostalgia as a distinct emotion. The measures are familiarity and autobiographical
  salience.
- Retrospective report reliability. The author states the postscan memory survey was probably more
  susceptible to false memories and distortion than immediate collection would be.
