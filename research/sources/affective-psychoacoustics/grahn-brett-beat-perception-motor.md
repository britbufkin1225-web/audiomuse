# Rhythm and Beat Perception in Motor Areas of the Brain

- Source ID: `grahn-brett-beat-perception-motor`
- Class: scholarly work (peer-reviewed primary research)
- Authors: Jessica A. Grahn, Matthew Brett
- Publication: Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 19 (5), 893–906, 2007
- Identifier: DOI 10.1162/jocn.2007.19.5.893
- Stable locator: https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2007.19.5.893
- Retrieval: full text retrieved

## Supports

- Rhythms with integer-ratio relationships between intervals and regular perceptual accents were
  reproduced more accurately, and elicited higher activity in the basal ganglia and supplementary
  motor area, than rhythms without those properties.
- The effect was consistent across levels of musical training; musicians showed additional activation
  in premotor cortex, cerebellum, and the SMAs that was unrelated to rhythm type.
- The authors' conclusion: in addition to their role in movement production, the basal ganglia and
  SMAs may mediate beat perception.
- Perceptual accent can arise from temporal structure alone, with pitch and loudness held constant.
  The paper gives the relevant example that an onset not closely followed by another onset is
  perceived as accented, as is the final onset of a run of two or three.

## Does not settle

- Emotion. No affective measure was taken, and this source must not be cited for any claim about
  pleasure, arousal, or groove.
- Causation. This is correlational fMRI plus a behavioural reproduction task.
- Whether the same regions mediate beat perception for the highly syncopated or fragmented material
  that temporal-DSP work produces; the stimuli were constructed rhythmic sequences.
