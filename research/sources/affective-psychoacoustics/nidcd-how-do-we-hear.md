## Supports

- The ordered hearing pathway: sound waves enter the outer ear and travel through the ear canal to the
  eardrum; the eardrum vibrates and transmits vibrations to the malleus, incus, and stapes, which
  amplify them; the bones send amplified vibrations to the fluid-filled cochlea, which the basilar
  membrane divides into upper and lower sections.
- Vibrations create a travelling wave along the basilar membrane, and hair cells riding that wave bend
  their stereocilia, opening channels that admit chemicals and create an electrical signal.
- Place coding by position: hair cells near the wide end of the cochlea detect higher-pitched sounds,
  and those closer to the centre detect lower-pitched sounds.
- The auditory nerve carries the electrical signal to the brain.

## Does not settle

- Anything about emotion, reward, or affect. This source establishes the transduction pathway only.
- The central auditory pathway above the auditory nerve. Brainstem nuclei, inferior colliculus, medial
  geniculate, and cortex are not described here.
- Phase locking, tuning curves, or the two-mechanism account of frequency coding; those come from
  `purves-neuroscience-auditory-system`.
