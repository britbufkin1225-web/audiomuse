# Houston Musical Cartography

A sourced chronology of Houston music infrastructure, written to support the `history-culture` nodes
rather than to replace them. Nodes hold durable concepts and typed relationships; this document holds
sequence, evidence, and the points where the evidence disagrees with itself.

## How to read it

Every entry is a claim with a registered source ID and a confidence marker. The markers are fixed:

| Marker | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `documented` | Stated by an institutional archive, a signed reference entry, or a contemporary record whose text was retrieved. |
| `reported` | Stated consistently by journalism or by accounts whose full text could not be retrieved in this phase. |
| `recollected` | Derived from oral history, interview, or participant memory. |
| `disputed` | Sources conflict; the conflict is recorded in the register below and no version is adopted. |
| `approximate` | The period is supported but the precise date is not. |

Nothing here is stronger than its marker. A repeated story does not become `documented` by being
repeated, and an institutional source is not automatically right when a participant contradicts it —
it is simply a different class of evidence, recorded as such.

## The layered model

```text
PLACE -> INSTITUTION / INFRASTRUCTURE -> PEOPLE -> MUSICAL PRACTICE / TECHNIQUE
      -> TECHNOLOGY / PLAYBACK / PRODUCTION -> CIRCULATION -> CULTURAL INFLUENCE
```

The ordering is the argument. Houston rap in the 1990s is legible because Houston already had rooms,
engineers, catalogs, retail, and duplication capacity. A year-artist-album list would show the same
records and explain none of that. The `houston-musical-cartography` node holds the model itself.

## 1. Recording and label infrastructure, 1941-1973

- **1941, October** — Bill Quinn opens the Houston facility later known as Gold Star and then SugarHill.
  `tsha-sugarhill-recording-studios` — documented
- **1947-1950** — Lightnin' Hopkins records at least thirty-six songs for Quinn.
  `tsha-sugarhill-recording-studios` — documented
- **1948** — Bill Holford establishes Audio Company of America; a dedicated studio opens in 1950 and
  moves through a documented sequence of Houston addresses into the 1990s.
  `tsha-aca-recording-studio` — documented
- **1948, May 5** — KCOH begins broadcasting; after its 1953 purchase by a group under Robert C. Meeker
  it is described as the first Black-owned radio station in Texas and programs to Houston's African
  American community for decades. `black-enterprise-kcoh` — documented for the broadcast and purchase
  dates, reported for the "first in Texas" characterization
- **1949** — Don Robey establishes Peacock Records, named for his Fifth Ward club, the Bronze Peacock.
  `tsha-duke-peacock-records` — documented
- **1953, April** — Robey acquires full control of Duke, with the contracts of Bobby "Blue" Bland,
  Junior Parker, and Johnny Ace; operations move that year to 2809 Erastus Street.
  `tsha-duke-peacock-records` — documented
- **1950s-1960s** — Robey records at ACA and masters at Gold Star, establishing the division between
  recording, mastering, and pressing that the Houston system keeps.
  `tsha-duke-peacock-records`, `tsha-aca-recording-studio` — documented
- **1968** — International Artists leases and then buys the Gold Star facility, recording the 13th
  Floor Elevators and Red Krayola. `tsha-sugarhill-recording-studios` — documented
- **1973, May 23** — Robey sells the Duke-Peacock holdings to ABC-Dunhill.
  `tsha-duke-peacock-records` — documented

The layer this establishes: Black-owned catalogs, commercial rooms, named engineers, and a working
division of technical labor, all in place decades before any rap economy existed.

## 2. School and community music, 1968-1978, and its later recirculation

- **1968** — Conrad "Prof" Johnson brings funk and soul arrangements into the Kashmere High School
  stage band in the Fifth Ward, after a 1967 Otis Redding concert; the band wins national awards and
  tours internationally until his 1978 retirement. `ida-thunder-soul-kashmere` — documented
- **2006** — A two-disc reissue of the band's recordings appears; an NPR story about it reaches a
  filmmaker. `ida-thunder-soul-kashmere` — documented
- **2008-2011** — Alumni reunite; the documentary *Thunder Soul* premieres in 2010 and reaches theaters
  in 2011. `ida-thunder-soul-kashmere` — documented

The loop — original recordings, sampling, reissue, film, new listeners — is recorded in
`music-archiving` as a demonstration that preservation actively recirculates material. No influence
between this material and chopped and screwed practice is asserted, because no source asserts one.

## 3. Independent and DIY record economies, 1978-1985

- **1978-1985** — Really Red operates as one of Houston's first punk bands and forms C.I.A. Records,
  a DIY label whose records are held today in the same university archive as the city's hip hop
  collections. `uh-cia-records-finding-aid` — documented for the archival holding, reported for the
  band and label chronology

This entry exists to record that Houston sustained self-released record economies outside rap in the
same period. AudioMuse explicitly asserts no influence between punk tape practice and later Houston
DJ practice; conceptual similarity is not evidence of lineage, and no retrieved source claims one.

## 4. Rap infrastructure before the mature Screw practice, 1986-1992

- **1986-1987** — K-Rino organizes the South Park Coalition around the South Park neighborhood; the
  collective operates without radio airplay or major-label backing and marks twenty-five years in 2012.
  `houston-press-spc-anniversary` — disputed on the founding year, reported on the independence
- **Late 1980s** — Mike Dean enters Houston's music scene; by the early 1990s he is working extensively
  with Rap-A-Lot alongside N.O. Joe as engineer and producer at once, favoring played instrumentation
  over direct sampling and later adopting MPC hardware. `npr-microphone-check-mike-dean` — recollected
  (participant interview)
- **1987-1988** — UGK forms in Port Arthur and releases the cassette *The Southern Way* on the Houston
  label Bigtyme Recordz in 1988; a Jive deal follows in 1992. `museum-gulf-coast-ugk`,
  `tsha-pimp-c` — documented
- **1992 or 1993** — Shawn and Aaron Brauch found Pen & Pixel after working at Rap-A-Lot, where outside
  artwork requests had begun arriving. `complex-pen-and-pixel`, `uh-pen-and-pixel-records-finding-aid`
  — disputed on the year

Rap-A-Lot's own founding year is deliberately absent. No source retrieved in this phase establishes
it, and the widely repeated year is not adopted on repetition alone.

## 5. DJ Screw, 1971-2000

- **1971, July 20** — Robert Earl Davis Jr. is born in Bastrop, Texas; he lives in Smithville and Los
  Angeles as a child and moves to Houston at about age ten. `tsha-dj-screw` — documented
- **1989** — He begins working as a disc jockey, often working on tapes until three or four in the
  morning. `tsha-dj-screw` — documented
- **circa 1990-1992** — The mature slowed-and-chopped practice emerges. No first tape and no single
  invention date is asserted. `tsha-dj-screw`, `walker-dj-screw-biography` — approximate
- **Nickname origin** — Two incompatible accounts circulate. `tsha-dj-screw` — disputed
- **Practice** — Two copies of a record are spun, new rhythms are chopped in, local rappers freestyle
  over the tracks, and the recording is slowed on tape. `walker-dj-screw-biography` — documented
  (publisher record); the same combination of slowing and repetition of beats, words, and phrases is
  stated by a University of Houston curator in `uh-news-dj-screw-exhibit` — documented
- **Sales** — Tapes are made and sold from a house on Greenstone Street on the south side; more than
  three hundred original mixtapes are recorded. `uh-stories-dj-screw` — documented; reported daily
  volumes of ten to fifteen thousand copies are participant claims — recollected
- **1996, June 27** — The June 27th freestyle session is recorded, described as marking a participant's
  birthday. `texas-standard-june-27` — reported; the date is repeated independently across public
  radio, magazine, and university-exhibit coverage whose full text could not be retrieved here
- **1996 or 1998** — Screwed Up Records & Tapes begins. `tsha-dj-screw` places the store, label, and
  business in Houston in 1996; widely circulated community accounts instead give a February 1998
  storefront opening on Cullen Boulevard, which no source registered in this phase establishes —
  disputed, with the 1998 version unverified
- **2000, November 16** — DJ Screw dies of a drug overdose. `tsha-dj-screw` — documented

## 6. The Screwed Up Click and 1998

- **Mid-1990s** — Rappers freestyle while Screw records; participants named by the University of
  Houston include C-Note, Will-Lean, the Botany Boyz, Lil' Flip, Big Pokey, Big Hawk, Big Moe, Fat Pat,
  and Lil' Keke. `uh-stories-dj-screw` — documented
- **1998, February 3** — Patrick Lamont Hawkins, known as Fat Pat, born December 4, 1970 and one of the
  original members of the Screwed Up Click, is fatally shot in Houston after visiting a promoter's
  apartment to collect payment for a performance; his debut album is released two weeks later.
  `tsha-fat-pat` — documented
- **2006** — His older brother, John Edward Hawkins, known as Big Hawk, is shot and killed.
  `tsha-fat-pat` — documented

These events are recorded because they shape the network's later history and its memorial
relationship to the tapes, not as biographical detail. No circumstance beyond the sourced facts is
inferred.

## 7. Circulation, 1990-2003

- **Through 2003** — Southwest Wholesale operates in Houston as an independent rap-focused distributor
  for twenty-seven years, among the largest in that field; layoffs begin January 30, 2003, and the
  collapse is attributed at the time to internet distribution and bootlegging.
  `allhiphop-southwest-wholesale-closing` — documented; its 1976 founding is reported but not verified
- **By 1995** — Pen & Pixel moves to a five-thousand-square-foot Houston office park facility; it
  produces roughly twenty thousand covers for clients including No Limit, Cash Money, and Suave House
  artists before closing in 2003. `complex-pen-and-pixel` — documented, with the cover count approximate
- **Ongoing** — Screw tapes circulate outside any distributor: sold directly, catalogued by name and
  number, and copied informally by sellers with no arrangement with Screw.
  `popula-screw-tape-records` — documented
- **By 2019** — The shop sells CDs rather than cassettes. `popula-screw-tape-records` — documented

## 8. Continuation, 1997 onward

- **1997** — Swishahouse is founded on Houston's north side; the sourcing available here names Michael
  "5000" Watts, OG Ron C, and G-Dash, while other accounts name only the first two as founders.
  `black-enterprise-michael-watts` — documented on the year, disputed on the lineup
- **Late 1990s-2000s** — Watts hosts a late-night radio show playing his own mixtapes; Paul Wall, Mike
  Jones, Slim Thug, and Chamillionaire are the artists most associated with the label's commercial
  success. `black-enterprise-michael-watts` — documented, with the station's frequency disputed
- **2026, January 30** — Michael "5000" Watts dies at fifty-two. `black-enterprise-michael-watts` —
  documented

Swishahouse is recorded as continuation *and* divergence: a related technique carried by a different
circulation system — radio, retail CDs, national release — rather than by hand-sold cassettes.

## 9. Preservation, 2010 onward

- **2010** — The Houston Hip Hop Research Collection is envisioned at the University of Houston
  Libraries, curated by Julie Grob. `uh-houston-hip-hop-research-collection`,
  `uh-stories-dj-screw` — reported on the founding year, documented on the curatorship and holdings
- **2012** — The exhibit "DJ Screw and the Rise of Houston Hip-Hop" is shown at M.D. Anderson Library
  through September 21. `uh-news-dj-screw-exhibit` — documented
- **Ongoing** — Described holdings include DJ Screw's papers, photographs, equipment, sound recordings,
  and tapes, plus material from the Geto Boys, the Screwed Up Click, Fat Pat, and HAWK; the Screw
  papers are the most requested collection in the archive. `uh-stories-dj-screw`,
  `uh-dj-screw-collection-finding-aid` — documented
- **Also held** — Pen & Pixel business records and C.I.A. Records material, and, at the Houston Public
  Library, a municipal archive for African American history.
  `uh-pen-and-pixel-records-finding-aid`, `uh-cia-records-finding-aid`,
  `hpl-african-american-history-research-center` — documented

## Representation: who is and is not here

The independent review identified a representation gap in the middle period. Phase 12C addressed it by
research rather than by inclusion, and the outcome is mixed and stated plainly.

- **Lyn Collins** — Excluded. She was proposed as a Houston figure; widely reported biographical accounts place
  her upbringing in Abilene, Texas, not Houston. Her recorded work is enormously significant as
  later sampling material, but placing her in a Houston cartography would be a geographic error. This
  is recorded as a correction, not an omission.
- **Yolanda Adams** — Not added. Houston birth, upbringing, and gospel training are consistently
  reported, and the continuity with Black Houston church and vocal infrastructure is exactly the kind
  of link this cartography wants. No institutional or reference-encyclopedia source was located within
  this phase, so she is named here as a researched candidate rather than written into the chronology.
- **Choice** — Not added as a node. Accounts describe her as an early female artist on Rap-A-Lot who
  moved to Houston from San Antonio in the late 1980s. Available sourcing is weak, and the frequently
  attached superlatives are exactly the kind of claim this phase refuses to promote. Recorded as a
  research target with a named strongest lead: Roni Sarig's *Third Coast* (`sarig-third-coast`).

The gap is therefore narrowed but not closed, and it is closed for the wrong reason if it is closed by
adding names without evidence. Phase 12D should pursue Yolanda Adams and Choice with archival and
scholarly sourcing.

## Deliberately deferred

Not attempted in this phase, and not to be inferred from it: a full slab culture and car-audio study
(`wilkins-welcome-2-houston` is the registered entry point); complete Houston punk, Tejano, zydeco,
and country histories; a full UGK and Port Arthur history; exhaustive S.U.C. biographies; a Houston
club-network study; sampling genealogy; and any geographic visualization or interface work.

## Future requirement: claim confidence as data

This document carries confidence markers in prose because the repository's schemas record provenance
as a source ID and nothing else. That is sufficient for a phase this size and will not stay
sufficient. A future claim model would need to distinguish, as stored fields rather than as sentences:

```text
confidence          how strongly the evidence supports the claim
evidence type       archive, reference entry, journalism, oral history, participant testimony
corroboration       how many independent sources support it
chronology precision exact date, month, year, range, or era
disputed            whether sources conflict, and where
retrieval status    whether the cited text was actually read
```

Phase 12C deliberately does not change any schema to add these. The requirement is recorded so that a
later phase can implement it against real content, and this phase preserves enough provenance — every
source note carries a retrieval status, a supports list, and a does-not-settle list — for that model to
be applied retroactively rather than re-researched.
